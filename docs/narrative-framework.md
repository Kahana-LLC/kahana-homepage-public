## Problem

*What is broken today for enterprises? For whom? What does it cost (risk, productivity, compliance, cost)?*

### The situation

**Market reality:** Security has moved to the browser; most access controls have not. Employees, contractors, and partners increasingly work directly in SaaS, often from devices the company does not own or manage. Models built around corporate laptops, VPNs, and network boundaries struggle to govern this world.

As orgs rely more on contractors and distributed teams, **secure access onboarding** is slow, expensive, and complex: shipping laptops or provisioning VDI delays work and raises cost, while **unmanaged browser access** drives compliance and data-loss risk — a growing gap between how work happens and how security is enforced.

**Core problem:** The browser is the new operating system for enterprise work (SaaS, internal tools, collaboration, AI workflows), yet security often lacks **consistent control inside that environment**. Root cause: uncontrolled growth of apps, extensions, and AI tools in the browser without centralized governance — “SaaS sprawl” — making policy enforcement, monitoring, and data protection hard, especially on third-party devices. Teams end up juggling restrictive rules, costly provisioning, or fragmented tools that never add up to a coherent control layer.

### Contractor / operational tension (one-pager)

**Contractor onboarding has become a security and operational bottleneck.**

- Security teams still **ship laptops or provision VDI** to contractors because **unmanaged browsers** cannot enforce corporate policies.
- Each contractor device adds **hardware cost**, **configuration overhead**, and **delays** that slow project kickoff and increase operational burden.
- **Browser and device management** approaches often **fail to enforce consistent controls** on third-party devices.

### Who feels it most (ICP)

- **Enterprise security teams** balancing governance with speed of access.
- **Organizations with heavy SaaS adoption**, **contractor / partner access**, and **distributed work**.
- **Teams paying the cost** of laptops, VDI, or **unmanaged browser access** (friction vs. risk).

**One-pager audience:** Built for **enterprise security and IT architecture leaders** responsible for **how contractors interact with corporate SaaS**. Oasis supports teams that need to **onboard external collaborators quickly** while maintaining strict governance over **identity, session behavior, and data access** — without provisioning devices or managing virtual desktops; **extend corporate policies into contractor browser sessions**.

### Why now

SaaS and contractor workforces are growing; the browser is effectively the **enterprise workspace**. Companies that stay purely device-centric face rising cost, slow onboarding, and policy gaps on unmanaged devices. Orgs that treat the browser as a **first-class security surface** can onboard in hours vs. weeks, enforce consistent policies, and reduce operational load from hardware and virtual desktops. The shift is already underway.

**Market context (one-pager):** *When work moves into SaaS, governance must move into the browser.*

- Modern work happens in **SaaS via the browser**, not only on traditional corporate networks.
- **VPNs, device management, and VDI** were built for **controlled endpoints** and struggle to scale with **distributed** workforces.
- **Shadow SaaS**, **browser extensions**, and **AI tools** have expanded **attack surface inside browser sessions** — strategies must **control how work happens in the browser**.

### Market Reality

**Security moved to the browser. Most access controls didn’t.**

Enterprise security teams are dealing with a fundamental shift in how work happens. Employees, contractors, and partners increasingly operate directly in SaaS applications, often from devices the company does not own or manage. Traditional security models — built around corporate laptops, VPNs, and network boundaries — struggle to govern this environment.

As organizations rely more heavily on contractors and distributed teams, onboarding secure access has become slow, expensive, and operationally complex. Shipping laptops or provisioning VDI infrastructure delays productivity and inflates costs, while unmanaged browser access introduces compliance and data-loss risks. The result is a widening gap between how modern work happens and how security is enforced.

### The Core Problem

**The browser has become the new operating system — but it’s largely unmanaged.**

Most enterprise work now lives inside the browser: SaaS apps, internal dashboards, collaboration tools, and increasingly even AI workflows. Yet security teams still lack consistent control over what happens inside that environment.

The root cause is the uncontrolled growth of applications, extensions, and AI tools running in the browser without centralized governance. This “SaaS sprawl” makes it difficult to enforce policies, monitor activity, or protect sensitive data — especially when users access systems from third-party devices.

Security teams are left juggling workarounds: restrictive access rules, costly device provisioning, or fragmented tools that fail to provide a coherent control layer.

### Why Existing Approaches Fall Short

**Today’s options force a trade-off between security and usability.**

Many organizations attempt to solve this problem using traditional device-centric approaches. Corporate laptops and VDI environments can provide strong control, but they are expensive to provision and slow to scale — especially for short-term contractors or external partners.

Enterprise browser tools and secure access platforms attempt to address parts of the challenge. Some integrate with identity providers or offer policy enforcement features, but many struggle to balance usability with consistent governance across managed and unmanaged devices.

Consequently, teams often face an uncomfortable compromise: either accept operational friction and rising infrastructure costs, or allow inconsistent browser access that introduces security and compliance risks.

### Our Approach

**A managed enterprise browser designed for modern SaaS access.**

Oasis is a managed enterprise browser built specifically for the way modern organizations access software. Instead of relying on device ownership as the control layer, Oasis places governance directly inside the browser environment where work actually happens.

Security teams can enforce unified browser policies across corporate and third-party devices, ensuring consistent protection for applications, data, and AI usage. The platform integrates cleanly with identity and security stacks — including systems such as Okta and enterprise DLP solutions — so existing policies flow naturally into the browsing environment.

The outcome is a modern security model that combines strong governance with a user experience people actually want to use, enabling faster onboarding and smoother access for contractors and distributed teams.

### Why Status Quo Is No Longer Optional

**The organizations that master browser security will move faster.**

As SaaS adoption accelerates and contractor workforces grow, the browser has effectively become the new enterprise workspace. Companies that continue to rely on device-centric security models will struggle with rising costs, slow onboarding, and increasing policy gaps across unmanaged devices.

Meanwhile, organizations that treat the browser as a first-class security platform gain a significant operational advantage. They can onboard contractors in hours rather than weeks, enforce consistent policies across environments, and reduce the operational burden associated with device provisioning and virtual desktops.

The shift is already underway — and companies that adapt early will operate with greater speed, security, and control.

### Our Point of View

**Security should live where work actually happens.**

The future of enterprise security is not about locking down devices — it’s about governing the environments where work takes place. Today, that environment is the browser.

We believe organizations need a control layer designed specifically for SaaS-centric work. One that integrates with the existing security stack, enforces consistent policies across all users, and eliminates the operational overhead of device-centric access models.

By bringing governance directly into the browser, security teams can protect data, enable flexible work, and support modern operating models without forcing organizations to choose between usability and control.
